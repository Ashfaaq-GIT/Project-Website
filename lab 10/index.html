This lab focused on building and programming the Adabox car with a Time of Flight imaging sensor. The car uses the VL53L5CX ToF imager to observe distance values across an 8x8 grid, compute left and right mean distances, and automatically avoid obstacles while still responding to Bluetooth driving commands.

Overview

The ToF sensor provides 64 distance readings arranged in an 8x8 grid. For obstacle detection, the grid is divided into left and right halves, and the average distance for each half is computed. A threshold of 300 mm is used. If the left side is blocked, the car turns right. If the right side is blocked, the car turns left. If both sides are blocked, the car stops and waits for the next command. Description

In this experiment, I assembled the Adabox car, connected the XIAO nRF52840 Sense board, SparkFun Qwiic motor driver, and VL53L5CX SparkFun ToF imager sensor, then uploaded the provided sketch to the board. The program used BLE commands for manual control while adding an obstacle-detection layer during forward motion. The Serial Monitor displayed live left and right mean values in millimeters, allowing me to observe how the car interpreted the environment and responded automatically.

When an object was detected on the left side, the car turned right and continued moving forward. When an object was detected on the right side, the car turned left. When both sides were blocked, the car stopped completely until another Bluetooth command was received. This confirmed that the ToF imaging sensor and obstacle avoidance logic were working as expected. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}
